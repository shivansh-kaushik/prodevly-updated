This section covers how the public home page is built. It applies to whichever design direction is chosen. Between directions, only the totem geometry, the materials, the pose tables and the field colours change. The stage, the scroll follower, the quality tiers and the build gates stay the same.

2. Home page sections

Page order matches the previews: navbar, hero, partners, services, projects, pricing, developers, contact and footer. Section ids are top, partners, services, work, pricing, team and contact. Six sections carry data-chapter and own a 3D station: hero, services, work, pricing, team and contact. Partners and the footer have no station.

Data rules

  • One loader, getHomeContent() in src/services/public/home.ts, reads the published partners, showcase projects, pricing plans and developer profiles. It uses explicit field projections and .lean(), sorts by each record's order field and returns plain DTOs. It never selects ledger, payment, pay or contact fields.
  • A section whose list is empty returns null. Its nav link and its 3D station go with it, because the nav is built from the same data and the stage reads the rendered [data-chapter] elements. Hidden sections leave no gap.
  • Static copy lives in src/content/*.ts as typed constants: hero, services, process, nav, site details and footer. Components hold no literal copy. The team confirms the drafts at the M2 and M3 gates.
  • The page is statically generated with export const revalidate = 3600. Publishing or unpublishing content revalidates its content tag (see the admin plan), so edits appear on the next request. cacheComponents stays off. If MongoDB cannot be reached, the loader throws. At build time that fails the build, and during revalidation Next keeps serving the last good page.

Navbar

  • Content. The ProDevly wordmark, which links to the top. Anchors to Services, Work, Pricing, Team and Contact. A "Start a project" button that scrolls to the form. On phones the bar holds the wordmark, a compact CTA and a menu button.
  • Data. Labels come from src/content/nav.ts. A link appears only when its section rendered.
  • Behaviour. Anchors call lenis.scrollTo with the bar height as the offset, and section[id] has scroll-margin-top as the no-JS fallback. The current-section marker is a data attribute that changes when the rendered chapter index changes. It is never updated every frame. The phone menu is a native <dialog> opened with showModal() and portalled to body, so a blurred or transformed header cannot squash it. lenis.stop() runs while the menu is open. A skip link to #main comes first in the tab order.
  • 3D beat. None. The bar sits above the stage on a solid field background.

Hero

  • Content. The H1 "Web apps, SaaS and custom software, built end to end." It is followed by one plain sentence on who ProDevly works with: founders, growing businesses and agencies, in India and abroad. The primary CTA is "Start a project". The secondary link is "See our work" when showcase projects exist and "What we build" otherwise.
  • Data. src/content/home.ts. The hero shows no numbers.
  • Rendering. The H1 is in the server HTML and paints in the first frame, with no opacity or clip entrance. The hero poster (an AVIF of the live scene in the same composition) fills the stage zone until the canvas renders its first frame. The canvas then crossfades in over 400 ms. If the poster covers more of the screen than the H1, it becomes the LCP element, so it is preloaded with fetchpriority="high" and kept under the size budget.
  • 3D beat. The hero totem rises into its pose through the same follower, with no separate intro animation. It then idles with a slow breathing offset and pointer tilt. On phones the totem sits centred between the headline in the top third and the CTA card at the bottom.

Partners

  • Content. A single band of partner logos under the heading "Partners", with each name as accessible text. A logo links to the partner's site when a URL is set.
  • Data. The partners collection. A record shows only when it is published and its permissionConfirmed flag is true, and the admin form will not publish without that flag. Logos are SVGs, sanitised on upload. They render in the section's ink colour through a CSS mask so the band reads as one set. On fine pointers, hovering a logo shows its original colours.
  • Empty state. The band is hidden. It has no placeholder logos and no "trusted by" line.
  • 3D beat. No station. The band passes during the travel between the hero and services stations, so the totem is moving while the logos scroll by. When the band is hidden, that travel gets shorter.

Services

  • Content. The six services are Web apps, SaaS products, Custom software, Mobile apps, UI/UX design, and Cloud and DevOps. Each has a one-sentence description, three to five included items and stack chips. Under the services sit a four-step "How we work" strip (Scope, Design, Build, Support) and the list of technologies ProDevly works in.
  • Data. src/content/services.ts, changed by pull request. These are statements about what ProDevly offers, and the team confirms them.
  • 3D beat. One becomes many, the signature beat in every direction. The hero pieces separate and recombine into one piece or group per service. On fine pointers, hovering or focusing a service row lifts the matching piece. On touch, the row nearest the viewport centre is the lit one.

Projects (Work)

  • Content. One card per published showcase project. A card has the title, the client display name when one is set, a one-paragraph summary, the services delivered, stack chips, a cover image through next/image and a live link when one is set.
  • Data. The showcaseItems collection, kept separate from the internal projects ledger. A showcase record may store an internal project id, but the public loader never follows it.
  • Empty state. The section is hidden. "Sample project" cards exist only in the previews.
  • 3D beat. One 3D piece per published project, up to six. Further projects appear only in the DOM list. The pieces line up and step forward in turn as their cards pass the stage zone. Each step is bound to its card's measured span. The 3D carries no project text, so titles and images stay in the DOM.

Pricing

  • Content. One card per plan with its name, who it suits, what it includes, a price line and a CTA. The CTA scrolls to the form with that plan pre-selected.
  • Data. The pricingPlans collection. A plan stores either an amount and a currency, formatted with Intl.NumberFormat, or a "quoted" flag that shows "Quoted after a scoping call". Only published plans render, from one to four.
  • Empty state. The section is hidden.
  • 3D beat. One element per plan, growing in size with plan order. Depending on the direction these are plates, plinths or totems with more pieces. The count and order follow the data.

Developers (Team)

  • Content. A grid of people, each with a photo (or an initial monogram when there is no photo), name, role, a two-line bio, skill chips, and GitHub and LinkedIn links when they are set.
  • Data. Published developerProfiles records with consentConfirmed set. These are separate from admin accounts, so the loader never reads pay, email or permissions.
  • Empty state. The section is hidden.
  • 3D beat. One small piece per person, up to eight. Each piece carries the person's initial as a maker's mark, drawn into a canvas texture at runtime from the record's name. The 3D shows no faces.

Contact

  • Content. A short heading, the line "We read every enquiry and reply by email.", the form, and the company email as a mailto link from src/content/site.ts.
  • Fields. Name, email, company (optional), project type (the six services plus "Something else"), plan (optional, pre-selected from pricing, shown only when plans exist), timing (This month, This quarter, Just exploring), message, and the Web3Forms botcheck honeypot. There is no budget field with figures.
  • Submission. The browser POSTs to Web3Forms and, in parallel, sends the same payload to /api/leads. That route validates with the shared zod schema, rate-limits, screens for spam and stores the enquiry in MongoDB so the team can see it in the admin panel. The form reports success when either call succeeds. When both fail, it shows an inline error with a retry button and the mailto link. CSP connect-src and form-action include api.web3forms.com.
  • Accessibility. Native constraint validation runs first, then zod on submit. Each error is linked to its field with aria-describedby, focus moves to the first invalid field, and the success message is announced from a role="status" region.
  • 3D beat. The final station shows an Enter key, an empty top slot, a start call or a centred monogram, depending on the direction. A successful submit plays the direction's send gesture once through the stage store. With reduced motion there is no gesture, and the DOM message confirms the send.

Footer

  • Content. The wordmark, one plain line about ProDevly, anchor nav, the services list, the email address, social links (only those set in src/content/site.ts), a Privacy link, and the copyright line with the current year.
  • 3D beat. None. The footer has a solid ink field. Once no station is in view, the ticker stops calling advance() and the canvas wrapper switches to visibility: hidden, so no GPU work runs behind the footer.

No fabricated statistics

A number appears on the public site only when the team typed it into a record (a price, for example) or when it is computed from records at render time. Version one shows no counts at all. The site has no testimonials, ratings, awards, client counts, years in business or "trusted by" lines, and the admin panel has no collection that could supply them. Two checks in npm run verify enforce the rule.

  1. Source grep. scripts/check-public-copy.mjs scans src/content/** and any texture strings in src/three/** for these patterns: \d+\s*\+, \d+(\.\d+)?\s*/\s*(5|10)\b, \d+\s*% and \b\d+[kKmM]?\s+(clients|projects|years|countries|reviews|users|apps|developers)\b. It also flags the phrases trusted by, award, rated, testimonial, satisfaction, happy clients and years of experience, ignoring case. A match fails the build unless its line carries a // copy-ok: reason comment. Every exemption is printed for review.
  2. Rendered check. scripts/check-rendered-copy.ts starts the production build against an in-memory MongoDB that holds only the seed, which is three superadmins and no public content. It loads / and collects the visible text plus every alt and aria-label. Any digit fails the check, except the copyright year in the footer. A second run loads fixture records (one partner, one project, one plan and one developer) and asserts that every digit sits inside an element marked data-source="db". Elements marked data-copy-ok, such as a direction's label numbering, are allowed and listed in the output.

3. 3D stage architecture

One fixed canvas sits behind server-rendered DOM. The page is complete without it, and the canvas only adds the totems. The stage is mounted from src/app/(site)/page.tsx and never from a layout, so admin routes never load three.js.

Layers, back to front

  1. Section fields (z-index 0). Each section has an absolutely positioned .field child in its field colour. When one section's field slides over the next one's during scroll, the moving edge is the colour wipe. It runs on the compositor with no JavaScript, and it works without WebGL.
  2. Stage canvas (z-index 10). A wrapper with position: fixed, top: 0, height: 100lvh, contain: strict, pointer-events: none and aria-hidden="true". The canvas is transparent (alpha: true, scene.background = null) and uses NeutralToneMapping, which keeps brand hues accurate.
  3. Grain (z-index 15). A fixed 128-pixel tiled noise webp at about 5% opacity. It is static, so reduced motion needs no change.
  4. Content (z-index 20). Text, cards and the solid scrim cards.
  5. Navbar (z-index 40), with dialogs in the top layer.

This order only works when main and the section elements create no stacking context. They carry no transform, filter, opacity below 1, isolation, contain, will-change or positioned z-index. GSAP animates child elements only. A lint rule in scripts/check-stacking.mjs greps the section components for those properties.

Chapters anchored to measured DOM sections

  • Each station's centre is the scroll position at which its [data-chapter] section's centre meets the viewport centre. Centres are measured again on ScrollTrigger refresh, on a ResizeObserver(document.body) debounced to 150 ms, after document.fonts.ready and after the stage mounts. Nothing is measured inside the frame loop.
  • chapterFromY() maps a scroll position to a chapter float, interpolating linearly between centres. A station hold then applies smootherstep from 0.22 to 0.78 inside each segment. The target therefore parks while a section's copy is on screen and moves only during the scroll between sections.
  • Sections get their height from their content. At most two signature beats may use a CSS position: sticky pin of up to 120vh extra each. ScrollTrigger pin is not used, so there is no pin-spacer reflow.

Poses as data per aspect bucket

  • There are three buckets, chosen by aspect ratio and never by user agent. tall covers aspects below 0.8 (phones, and tablets in portrait). mid covers 0.8 up to 1.35. wide covers 1.35 and above. tall and wide are authored in src/three/poses/tall.ts and wide.ts. mid uses the wide poses, which the framing solver re-fits, and it may override single stations.
  • A pose holds { cam, aim, vFov, subject: { pos, rot, scale }, assembly, safe, env }. assembly is a record of 0-to-1 parameters, such as stacked to cluster, that drive every piece's transform. safe is the named safe rect, in viewport fractions, that the subject's projected bounds must fit. env holds the environment yaw and tint.
  • Poses are absolute, so any station can be missing and the stations on either side still interpolate correctly. Camera position and aim follow centripetal Catmull-Rom rails through the stations that are present, sampled with getPoint(i / (n - 1)) so the rail passes exactly through each station. Scalars lerp, rotations slerp and assembly parameters lerp.
  • Signature beats are continuous transforms of the same pieces, driven by assembly parameters. Nothing fades one mesh out while another fades in.
  • Every station changes at least two of these five: screen position across thirds, scale by 1.5x or more, camera angle or framing, material or colour, and form. The screenshot review checks this.
  • The DOM layout of each section reserves a stage zone that matches the safe rect, for example a spacer of about 46svh between headline and card on phones. ?debug=stage draws the safe rect and the projected subject bounds over the page so the two can be compared.

One clock for Lenis, GSAP and R3F

  1. One gsap.ticker callback runs each frame, with gsap.ticker.lagSmoothing(0). On tiers 1 and 2 gsap.ticker.fps(60) caps 90 Hz and 120 Hz phones.
  2. It calls lenis.raf(time * 1000), and lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update) keeps DOM reveals in step.
  3. The scroll store computes the target chapter from lenis.targetScroll. It reads Lenis's target, never its smoothed value, so the smoothing is not applied twice.
  4. stepFollowers(dt) advances the rendered chapter values.
  5. advance(time) runs on a Canvas with frameloop="never", and time is in seconds. The useFrame callbacks evaluate the poses, add the secondary springs and render.
  6. Labels projected onto the DOM are written after updateMatrixWorld(true), in the same tick.

Steps 4 to 6 are skipped when no station is in view or the tab is hidden. React never re-renders at scroll rate, and the store is a mutable module that holds no React state.

4. Motion rules

  • Scroll sets a target only. Scroll handlers write scroll.target and nothing else. No code writes a transform from a scroll event.
  • Follower. A critically damped smoothDamp on the chapter float. Wide and mid buckets use a smoothTime of 0.45 s and a maxSpeed of 1.6 chapters per second. The tall bucket uses 0.55 s and 1.3 chapters per second. The camera has a parallel follower on the same target with 0.15 s more smoothTime, so the subject drifts slightly inside the frame while travelling. Neither follower reads the other's output.
  • Catch-up. The speed cap is multiplied by 1 + 2 * max(0, gap - 1), where the gap is in chapters. Up to one chapter behind, the cap is unchanged. Two chapters behind, it triples.
  • Fade-cut. When the gap passes 2.5 chapters (an End key press, a nav click or a scrollbar drag), the canvas fades out over 150 ms. While it is invisible, both followers jump to half a chapter before the target. The canvas then fades back in over 200 ms and the followers glide the last half chapter. The first frame and a restored scroll position snap with no fade.
  • dt clamp. dt = Math.min(delta, 1 / 20) in every frame callback, so a tab switch or a hitch cannot cause a jump. All damping is time-based, so 30 Hz Low Power mode, 60 Hz and 120 Hz screens move at the same speed.
  • Never damp in series. The follower is the only smoothing between scroll and the main pose. Poses are pure functions of the rendered chapter. Secondary motion (breathing, tilt, lean, press and hover lift) is added as offsets, each with its own spring, after the pose is evaluated. None of it feeds back into the pose.
  • Scroll-driven channels. Scroll delta is clamped to 60 px per frame in either direction before it feeds anything. Lean from scroll velocity is limited to 5 degrees with a smoothTime of 0.3 s. Any spin driven by scroll is capped at 2.5 radians per second.
  • Pointer. Fine pointers only. Yaw is limited to 6 degrees and pitch to 4 degrees, with a smoothTime of 0.35 s. The environment rotates up to 0.2 radians with a smoothTime of 0.6 s. A hover lift moves a piece by up to 6% of its height, with a smoothTime of 0.25 s. The magnetic CTA moves by 30% of the pointer offset, up to 10 px, through gsap.quickTo over 0.45 s with power3.
  • Keyboard input (Keycap Colorway only). A keydown on the document presses a cap only when no input, textarea, select or contenteditable element has focus and no modifier key is held. The cap travels 4% of its height in 60 ms and returns with a smoothTime of 0.22 s. The handler never calls preventDefault.
  • Theme switches. Fields change at section edges in the DOM, so the wipe follows the scroll exactly. Text and background colours never interpolate. Each section sets its ink colour in CSS for its own field. The stage's environment tint follows the rendered chapter because it lights objects and never sits behind text. scripts/check-contrast.ts checks every field and ink pair against WCAG AA, which means 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text.
  • DOM reveals. ScrollTrigger reveals play once each. They animate transform and opacity on child elements over 600 ms with cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1) and a 60 ms stagger. The H1 has no reveal.
  • Reduced motion. Lenis falls back to native scroll, because respectReducedMotion is on by default. The target becomes the nearest station, with no holds and no travel. A station change is a cut. Canvas opacity dips to 0 over 150 ms, the pose switches, and opacity returns over 150 ms. Breathing, tilt, lean, the typing rhythm, the send gesture, the magnetic button and DOM reveals are all off, and content is visible from the start. The totems still render, and fields still change at section edges, since that is ordinary scrolling.

The follower is the same smoothDamp the previews use, so the motion the user approves in the previews is the motion that ships.

// src/three/rig/follower.ts  (called once per gsap.ticker frame, before advance)
import { smoothDamp } from '@/lib/motion/damp'      // same maths as the previews' common.js
import { scroll } from '@/lib/scroll-store'          // only scroll handlers write scroll.target
import { stageFade } from '@/three/rig/stage-fade'

const TUNING = {
  wide: { smoothTime: 0.45, maxSpeed: 1.6 },         // maxSpeed in chapters per second
  tall: { smoothTime: 0.55, maxSpeed: 1.3 },
}
const CUT_GAP = 2.5                                   // chapters
const subject = { value: 0, velocity: 0 }
const camera = { value: 0, velocity: 0 }              // parallel follower, never fed by subject
const out = { subject: 0, camera: 0 }                 // reused, nothing allocated per frame

export function stepFollowers(rawDt: number, bucket: 'wide' | 'tall') {
  const dt = Math.min(rawDt, 1 / 20)                  // a hitch or tab switch cannot cause a jump
  const target = scroll.target                        // chapter float with station holds applied
  if (scroll.snap) {                                  // first frame or restored scroll position
    subject.value = camera.value = target
    subject.velocity = camera.velocity = 0
    scroll.snap = false
  }
  const gap = Math.abs(target - subject.value)
  if (gap > CUT_GAP) stageFade.request()              // canvas fades out over 150 ms
  if (stageFade.isDark()) {                           // jump while invisible, glide the last half
    subject.value = camera.value = target - Math.sign(target - subject.value) * 0.5
    subject.velocity = camera.velocity = 0
    stageFade.release()                               // canvas fades back in over 200 ms
  }
  const { smoothTime, maxSpeed } = TUNING[bucket]
  const cap = maxSpeed * (1 + 2 * Math.max(0, gap - 1))   // catch-up when far behind
  smoothDamp(subject, target, smoothTime, dt, cap)
  smoothDamp(camera, target, smoothTime + 0.15, dt, cap)
  out.subject = subject.value
  out.camera = camera.value
  return out                                          // poses are pure functions of these
}

Unit tests in src/lib/motion/damp.test.ts prove three things. Speed never exceeds the cap without catch-up. The value never overshoots the target. Stepping at 30, 60 and 120 Hz gives positions within 1% of each other after one second.

5. Mobile

Designed composition

  • The phone layout is designed first and reviewed first. On tall screens the totem's centre sits 36 to 40% from the top. In the hero the headline takes the top third, and the CTA and copy sit on a solid card in the bottom 40%.
  • In signature stations the totem never drops below 0.85 opacity and fills at least 70% of the viewport width. It is never parked at a screen edge.
  • On tall screens the camera favours moves in depth: dolly-in, crane up and a 90-degree orbit. Two stations in a row never use the same move. The world moves around a centred subject, so nothing runs down one side of the screen.
  • Copy sits on a solid card in the section's field colour, at full opacity on phones and never below 0.92 anywhere. Text never sits directly on a 3D area.

Framing

  • camera.setViewOffset shifts the projection centre without turning the camera. The offset comes from the pose's safe rect, never from a constant. On wide screens the subject usually sits in the right third with copy on the left.
  • The vertical FOV is derived from a 50-degree horizontal FOV and clamped to 28 to 45 degrees. Beyond about 45 degrees, bevelled forms near the frame edge look visibly distorted. The solver then sets the camera distance so the subject's bounding sphere fits its safe rect with a 6% margin. On tall screens that pulls the camera back instead of widening the lens.
  • Framing is recomputed when the width changes or the height changes by more than 15% (an orientation change). Safari's toolbar showing or hiding never triggers it.

Quality tiers

The tier is chosen in StageMount before three.js is downloaded. It runs a WebGL2 probe that releases its context, checks the Save-Data setting and navigator.deviceMemory, and then runs getGPUTier() against the self-hosted benchmarks. Mobile results are capped at tier 2. At runtime, PerformanceMonitor with flipflops={3} only steps down. onDecline drops one tier (never below 1), and onFallback sets tier 1. If tier 1 stays below 30 fps for 5 s, the stage fades to the posters. The tier never steps up during a visit, and each tier has a fixed DPR. ?tier=0 to ?tier=3 forces a tier in dev and preview builds.

The glass or resin hero piece uses fake glass on every tier, made from a physical material with clearcoat, a slight opacity and an inner coloured core. Real transmission is ruled out because it samples a cleared buffer that cannot see the DOM field behind a transparent canvas.

Poster fallback

  • scripts/posters.ts renders the live scene through the dev route /dev/stage?station=hero&bucket=tall&still=1 with Playwright. It captures 390 by 844 and 1440 by 900 at 2x and encodes AVIF with sharp. The hero poster must be 120 KB or less and each station still 60 KB or less.
  • The hero poster is a <picture> with an aspect media query. Its box reserves the stage zone with aspect-ratio, so the handover causes no layout shift. On tier 0, station stills load lazily in their stage zones.
  • Posters are regenerated in the same commit as any change to poses or materials. npm run verify warns when files in src/three are newer than the posters.

iOS resize hardening

  • The canvas wrapper is fixed at 100lvh, so it never resizes when the toolbar collapses. Sections use svh minimums. The canvas never uses 100vh or dvh.
  • R3F is configured with resize={{ scroll: false, debounce: { scroll: 0, resize: 200 } }}. DPR is fixed per tier and never changes while scrolling, because every change reallocates the canvas.
  • Only one WebGL context exists on the site. On webglcontextlost the stage calls preventDefault(), shows the poster and remounts once on webglcontextrestored. A second loss sets tier 0 for the rest of the visit.
  • Geometries, materials and textures are disposed on unmount, and textures are 1024 pixels or smaller on phones.
  • Test devices are a non-Pro iPhone on current iOS Safari (including Low Power mode at 30 fps), a Snapdragon 7-series Android phone in Chrome, and an iPad.

Touch

  • R3F events are attached only for fine pointers, through eventSource={document.body} with eventPrefix="client". On coarse pointers the canvas handles no events. Hover targets are invisible low-poly proxies with raycast={meshBounds}.
  • On touch, the totems lean with clamped scroll velocity instead of pointer tilt. Device orientation is not used, because it triggers a permission prompt on iOS.
  • No feature depends on hover alone. Links and buttons are at least 44 px. Nothing sets touch-action: none, and Lenis syncTouch stays off, so native momentum scrolling is untouched.

The gate every milestone from M2 on must pass

  1. npm run verify passes. It runs tsc --noEmit, ESLint, check-public-copy, check-rendered-copy, check-stacking, check-contrast, knip and the import audit. The audit fails if a client file reaches models, the database or auth, or if an admin file reaches three, R3F, GSAP or Lenis.
  2. npm run shoot uses Playwright to capture 375 by 812, 768 by 1024 and 1440 by 900 at every station's hold position (?jump=station) and at the midpoint of every transition. It runs once with reduced motion off and once with it on. From M3 on it also captures 360 by 800 and 390 by 844. The shots are reviewed next to the approved preview frames for text over busy 3D, totem opacity and the two-of-five change rule.
  3. Lighthouse runs the mobile and desktop presets on a production build and must meet the budgets in section 6.
  4. axe runs through @axe-core/playwright on every captured state and must report zero serious or critical violations.
  5. The work is committed on the feature branch, with DESIGN.md updated in the same commit.
